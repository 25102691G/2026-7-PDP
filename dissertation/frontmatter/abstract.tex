\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
This dissertation studies whether personalized node-level privacy can improve GNN-based influence-maximization Coverage under heterogeneous requirements, compared with a uniform mechanism calibrated to the strictest policy. It also examines run-to-run variability.

The studied PDP v2 mechanism combines a fixed public identity domain and Registry, one-time personalized Bernoulli participation, stable overlapping slots, double clipping, and Gaussian optimization over all 2340 model coordinates. Projection constrains the final 32 feature-map weights after each update. Under explicit fixed-domain, locality, ideal-randomization, and release assumptions, the conditional analysis gives approximate epsilon bounds of 0.986940 for target 1 and 1.982093 for targets 2 and 4, with corresponding delta bounds 0.0000026894 and 0.00001. These bounds do not certify machine implementation or private evaluation.

Experiments use five datasets---Email, Bitcoin, LastFM, HepPh, and Facebook---and four configurations: Non-Private, Uniform1, PDP v2, and Uniform4. Each dataset/configuration cell reports three runs, giving 60 runs in total, summarized by Coverage mean and sample standard deviation. PDP v2 has higher mean Coverage than Uniform1 on all five datasets, with observed differences ranging from 4.938 to 14.762 percentage points. It remains below Non-Private on all five datasets, and exceeds Uniform4 only on LastFM. Its sample standard deviation is lower than Uniform1 on two datasets and higher on three. These descriptive three-run comparisons do not establish statistical significance, a general stability advantage, or independent confirmation. Parameter settings, individual outcomes, and source provenance are documented in the appendices.

Static analysis identifies large full-coordinate noise scales relative to the clipped signal bound. Historical controlled diagnostics show substantial Coverage changes across Gaussian paths with initialization and retention fixed. Outer sampling and evaluation randomness cannot explain all historical instability. A complete causal variance decomposition remains unavailable.

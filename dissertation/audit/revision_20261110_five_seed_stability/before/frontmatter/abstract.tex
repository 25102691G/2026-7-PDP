\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
Influence maximization selects a small seed set to reach many nodes in a network. Graph neural network training can expose sensitive node information. Strict uniform protection may leave heterogeneous budgets unused. This dissertation investigates whether heterogeneous node requirements can support better Coverage than a uniform strict policy, without assuming that personalization must improve utility.

The studied PDP v2 mechanism combines a fixed public identity universe and privacy Registry, personalized Bernoulli participation, stable overlapping slots, double gradient clipping, and Gaussian optimization over all 2340 model coordinates. A nonnegative projection acts on the final 32 weights after each update. Under explicit fixed-domain, locality, ideal-randomization, and model-release assumptions, the conditional analysis yields approximate per-node bounds of epsilon 0.986940 with delta 0.0000026894 for target 1, and epsilon 1.982093 with delta 0.00001 for targets 2 and 4. Targets 2 and 4 share the same bound. They do not certify machine implementation or private evaluation.

An independent confirmation study contains 2900 final analysis units across five fixed graphs: Email, Bitcoin, LastFM, HepPh, and Facebook. Per graph there are 200 PDP, 200 strict Uniform epsilon 1, 140 relaxed Uniform epsilon 4, and 40 Non-Private runs. The primary equal-weight mean difference between PDP v2 and strict Uniform is positive at 0.784540 percentage points, but the frozen one-sided p-value is 0.187467. The two-sided 95 percent confidence interval is minus 0.949294 to 2.518373 percentage points. This result supports neither a statistically established advantage nor the planned practically important improvement of 3 percentage points; it does not establish equivalence. Coverage below 50 percent occurs in 103 of 1000 PDP runs and 115 of 1000 strict Uniform runs, without an established stability advantage.

The findings separate conditional privacy feasibility from empirical utility improvement. They identify persistent low-tail losses, limited budget utilization, and uncertainty in high-dimensional private optimization as priorities for future research. Generalization beyond the fixed graphs, full-system privacy, and joint publication of multiple models remain outside the established guarantees.

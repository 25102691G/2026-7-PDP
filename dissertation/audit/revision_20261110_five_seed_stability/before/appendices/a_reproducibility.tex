\section{Statistical replication}
The editable draft includes a deterministic scalar-verification script. It checks the sealed output hashes, unique run identities, exact cell counts, Spread-to-Coverage arithmetic, mean, sample SD, range, strict-low-tail counts and intervals, and primary and secondary Welch contrasts. It never loads graph files or checkpoints and does not re-run the bootstrap.

The accompanying CSV retains all 2900 saved scalar rows with public initialization and provenance. Those rows remain an internal audit artifact, separate from the submission manuscript. This separation keeps engineering evidence accessible without inserting thousands of run identifiers into the dissertation body.

A repeated future study would reproduce the public protocol and the intended output distribution. It could not reproduce each secret path from a stored Gaussian seed, because that material is deliberately not recorded. Equal public initialization does not imply equal private randomness, and a hash confirms byte identity rather than a privacy theorem.

\section{Proof-review obligations}
The ideal proof has a complete archived chain for slot locality, sensitivity, adaptive Gaussian RDP, the stated conversion, and bidirectional one-time amplification. Before relying on it for a particular release, the student and supervisor should verify that all public auxiliary information is legitimately fixed, the decoded adjacency is applicable, slot isolation remains true, and the intended output is within the theorem.

End-to-end raw preprocessing, private-test release, unrestricted private seed identities, finite-precision randomization, timing/failure channels, and joint release of the research collection have no completed certificate in this dissertation. Any claim extending to those objects is \texttt{PROOF\_REVIEW\_REQUIRED}. The draft supplies no invented proof to close those gaps.
